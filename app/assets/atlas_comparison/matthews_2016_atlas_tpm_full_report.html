Errors are reanalysis minus published values. The diagonal bands are forced by the coordinates: when published TPM is zero, error = +2 × average log-expression; when reanalysis TPM is zero, error = −2 × average log-expression. Of the 48,704 pairs with absolute error >2, 21.4% contain an exact zero. Published TPM was ≥10 while reanalysis TPM was zero in 2,978 pairs; the reverse occurred in 4,361. Density plots clip only the outer 0.1% for readable axes; summary metrics use the full distribution.

Green denotes published 0 → reanalysis nonzero; red denotes published nonzero → reanalysis 0. An exact zero can mean no compatible fragments were assigned under that quantification model; it is not a universal biological absence threshold. Threshold bars therefore ask how large the value is on the nonzero side. There are 55,502 exact published 0 → reanalysis nonzero pairs and 15,231 published nonzero → reanalysis 0 pairs. At a nonzero-side threshold of 1 TPM these fall to 7,797 and 5,097; at 10 TPM, 4,361 and 2,978.

Each panel starts from one-to-one matched gene TPM transformed with log2(TPM + 1). The separate panels retain their own 500 highest-variance genes. The joint panel ranks each gene by the average of its variance within the published and reanalysis profiles, so a processing-wide mean offset cannot by itself select a gene. PCA centers the retained gene columns but does not scale them to unit variance. This is not the paper's raw-count VST PCA. Separate PCAs compare biological geometry; the joint PCA also exposes method-specific shifts.

The diagonal compares the same biological sample across processing methods. A diagonal maximum in each row argues against sample swaps.
